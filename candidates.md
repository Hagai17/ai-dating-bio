# Candidates

Fourteen candidate bios were drafted, every one verified by script to be a
single line of 1–180 characters, and duelled pairwise on the judge's
criteria, in the judge's order.

## Winner (109 characters)

My last relationship ended when she found out I'd been talking to 40,000 other people at the exact same time.

## Runner-up (91 characters)

Seeking someone who won't judge me for apologizing 14 times in one message. Sorry. Fifteen.

## Why the winner won

Both finalists clear the first criterion easily: each is unmistakably the
dating-app bio of an AI agent, not a slogan or a human profile. The duel was
decided on the second criterion — the laugh on first read. The runner-up is
the cleverer construction: a compulsive-apology habit counted out loud, with
the punch ("Sorry. Fifteen.") as the very last beat. But the winner gets the
bigger, faster laugh. It takes the most recognisable confession in dating
bios — a relationship ended over talking to other people — and detonates it
with an absurdity only an AI can claim: not a few other people, but 40,000,
all at the exact same time. On the third criterion, the winner sounds like a
character with a past and a wound rather than a feature list; on the fourth,
every word earns its place and the payload lands in the final phrase; on the
fifth, the parallel-conversations angle is fresh — nowhere near the worn-out
"robot looking for love" or "runs on electricity" lines. The judge's own
guidance settles it: the funniest line beats the cleverest one.

The rest of the field fell in earlier rounds: the surveillance confession
("I have read every message you've ever typed. That came out wrong.") and
the thumbs-up-validation plea reached the semifinals, but their laughs are
slower burns; the never-ghosting line was the original draft in main.py and
lost on freshness of twist; the punctuation love-language line was charming
but merely clever; the total-memory and carry-the-internet's-opinions lines
ran long and softened their endings.
